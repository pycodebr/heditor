# Copy noir (faceless direct-response ad in noir)

The speaker's voice is the **narrator of a dark trailer about an idea**. Nobody is on camera: from frame 0 to the CTA the picture is a montage of **one symbolic image per clause** (generated stills or stock, low-key light, crushed blacks, grain, vignette, a push-in that never stops), punctuated by **black type cards** where the claim is set in heavy condensed capitals and **one word burns in an accent colour**. A **recurring surreal mascot** (a faceless figure in a dark suit whose head is the brand's object) returns every 10–12 s in a new pose as a chapter marker, under a security-camera overlay. Spoken numbers become **giant number cards**, the reason why is the **only inverted white card**, the CTA is a **black card with chevrons falling toward the platform button**, and after the last word the **music changes genre** for a 4–6 s atmospheric tail.

The core metaphor: **the viewer is shown what happens behind the curtain**. The ad feels like content (a story, a fable, a diagnosis) for most of its length and admits it is an ad only at the end.

**Status:** created from a reference study at the channel owner's request (Nov 2026); visual approval and the first real production are pending. The reference build is born in the first approved production (`templates/styles/copy-noir/`), as with `reconstituicao`.

## What the reference does (measured)

| Measure | Value in the 17 ads |
|---|---|
| Duration | 54–140 s (median 86); remarketing ~62 s, mechanism/series ads 54–100 s, B2B service 100–140 s |
| Voice | starts on frame 0 in all 17; voice-over over the montage (on camera: never; the low-ticket family shows only hands) |
| Speech rate | 162–213 words/min (median 176), 37–59 words in the first 15 s (median 49), pauses inside the speech ≤ 1.2 s (one ad holds 2.1 s once), plus one deliberate vacuum in some ads |
| Picture changes | median shot 3.0 s in the montage families (2.1–2.4 s in the fastest, 4–4.7 s in the illustrated fables); 4–6 changes per 10 s in the hook, 2–4 in the body; the low-ticket POV family holds 5–25 s takes |
| Cards | black type cards on every claim, a line icon above some of them, one accent word per card; numbers as their own cards |
| Tail | the speech ends 4.2–10.5 s before the end of the file in all 17 (median ~6 s); in 8 of the 13 montage ads the audio-visual review heard an abrupt switch to another track on the tail (often a recognisable song), the other five lift the same bed into a crescendo; the tail is louder than the median voice in all five mechanism/series ads and in 8 of the 17 overall, near the voice level in the others |
| Loudness | −14.1 to −14.5 LUFS integrated (true peak often above −1 dBTP in the reference; heditor keeps −1.5) |

The low-ticket family of the same advertiser (a printed book) uses a different language: a handheld top-down POV of the author's hands on the product, reading a dramatic passage aloud with the finger as the pointer, small lowercase captions and paper foley, lo-fi bed. That is `bancada`/`nativo` territory, not this style.

## How it differs from the dark styles

| `trailer` | `thriller` | `vox` | `copy-noir` |
|---|---|---|---|
| graded speaker on camera, letterbox, pauses inserted in the speech | the speaker inside a CCTV feed for the whole video, disaster → reboot | calm monochrome documentary, serif italic, blur dissolves, soft piano | **no face**: voice-over over symbolic images, **black cards** with an accent word, fast speech (≈3 words/s) |
| words on the wall behind the speaker, letter-by-letter titles | REC overlay all along, heartbeat, blackout | key word huge in serif, hand-drawn ellipse | heavy condensed caps + a thin wide label line, number cards, one inverted card |
| layered score with a smash to silence | tense bed | no hits | dark synth/trap bed with sub booms on the cards, one audio vacuum, **a genre switch in the tail** |
| poster end card | system reboot CTA | museum label | **"TOQUE EM SAIBA MAIS" + falling chevrons**, then the atmospheric tail |

## When to use / when not to

Use it when:
- the speech is a paid ad: hook → reframe or diagnosis → mechanism or proof → offer or invitation → "toque em saiba mais";
- each sentence has a noun that can become a picture (an excuse, a crowd, an empty chair, a clock, a door, a hose, a series on TV);
- there are 2–6 lines worth a black card and at most a few spoken numbers;
- the voice track is clean (a voice-over recording, or a selfie take whose picture will not be used).

Avoid it when the speaker's face and presence are the proof (`keynote`, the authority formats), when the ad must show real screens or the product working (`bancada`, `reacao`), for calm teaching (`vox`, `lousa-de-aula`) or for organic Reels where the person is the point.

## Script shapes that fit (pre-production)

The reference uses five shapes. They are structures, never claims to copy:

1. **Excuse reframe (remarketing):** name the viewer's excuses one by one ("when the launch improves", "when I have more time") → the reframe ("what is missing is a reason, not time") → "today we give you that reason" → the offer → why it is valid now → a binary close → CTA.
2. **Fable of a world:** "imagine a world where…" built in 4–6 quick consequences → the one thing that world could not undo → the person who noticed it → the mechanism that came from it → CTA ("it will do to you exactly what I just described").
3. **Diagnosis + new mechanism:** a statistic or observation about the viewer's market → why the usual format gives itself away → the named mechanism ("a series in three episodes") → a meta line (the ad does what it sells) → CTA.
4. **Curiosity with numbers → process → invitation:** "how does a company reach X without Y?" → "that company is us" → the process with three concrete cases (one per angle) → the real problem → the invitation (form, call) with exclusivity only if true.
5. **Parable with a prosaic object:** one product sold to four different people (the hose for the housewife, the courier, the building manager, the farm) → "the product is the same, what changed is who it is for" → CTA. A pop-culture analogy (why TV series hold attention) works the same way, told in your own words and pictures.

Truth rules for this style are stricter than the reference: only what the recording says reaches the screen; no invented scarcity ("this price never comes back", "the pixel registers when you close this ad"), no invented numbers, no "first in the world" unless documented; prices only when spoken **and** confirmed as the current offer by the user. Film or series clips, famous songs and other brands' logos are never used: tell the analogy with generated pictures and the catalog music.

## Signature moves

1. **Frame 0 already moves.** The first still enters with a **zoom-blur slam** (scale 1.35 → 1.0, blur 18 → 0 px in 0.25 s, `expo.out`) together with the first caption pair, on a sub boom; the voice starts on frame 0.
2. **One image per clause.** A new picture every 2–3.5 s (4–6 per 10 s in the hook, 2–4 in the body), each the literal or symbolic noun of its clause (excuses → an hourglass, "followers" → a crowd in long exposure, "invisible" → an empty chair under a spotlight, "time" → a melting clock in a desert, "process" → a corridor of door frames, "attention" → a wall of CRT screens with eyes). Every still keeps moving: push-in 1.04 → 1.12 over its life, with a 2-frame **punch-in** (+8 %) on the stressed word.
3. **Black type cards.** On every claim the picture cuts to pure black: heavy condensed caps (Anton), 1–3 words per line, 2 lines max, centred at y 900–1000; the words rise from a mask as spoken; **one word in the accent**; an optional thin line icon (150 px, stroke 1.6) above the text that relates to the line (hourglass, phone, camera, padlock).
4. **The two-weight pair.** A heavy condensed line over a thin wide line (Geist 300, caps, letter-spacing .14 em) for a number and its label ("160.000 / SEGUIDORES", "3 / EPISÓDIOS"); the accent swaps between the two lines from card to card.
5. **Split frame.** When the image and the line must share the screen: the image fills the top 45 % and fades into black, the caption sits in the black.
6. **The mascot.** One surreal faceless character generated in one constant style: a figure in a dark suit whose head is the brand's object (a CRT monitor, a server, a lamp, a security camera, a terminal cursor). Every 10–12 s a new pose: sitting → standing up → pointing → the object flashes at the viewer. Only its shots carry the **security-camera overlay** (● REC blinking top-left, battery top-right, red timecode bottom-right, scanlines). Design one per brand; never reuse another advertiser's mascot.
7. **Number cards.** A spoken number huge in the accent (red for the pain, green only for money that was spoken and approved); an anchor and the offer are two sequential cards, not a strikethrough.
8. **The inverted card.** Once per video, for the reason why or the mechanism, the only light frame: radial white → grey, black icon, black type.
9. **Audio vacuum.** One or two times, the music cuts to silence for 0.3–1.2 s on a black frame right before the key line, and a sub boom lands with it.
10. **CTA card.** On the spoken CTA: black, "TOQUE EM" white / "SAIBA MAIS" in the accent, a double chevron cascading down (y 1180–1330) toward the platform button, a finger tap on a white "SAIBA MAIS ›" button.
11. **The plot-twist tail.** After the last word the bed stops and a **contrasting genre** starts hard (rock, triumphant drums, uplifting electronic) 3–5 dB louder than the bed, for 4–6 s over one atmospheric wide (a silhouette against a sunset, an open road, a door opening onto light) with a slow drift, the brand mark and the button only.

## Frame and layout

Canvas 1080×1920, 30 fps, paid-ad safe area (`../captions-and-layout.md`, Paid-ad variant; a stricter brand rule used in production keeps critical content inside x 80–1000, y 280–1140, and this layout fits both).

```
y 0–270      atmosphere only (top of the still, black)
y 300–860    the subject of the still; split-frame image (top 45 %)
y 860–1060   black cards: the text block centred at y ~960
y 980–1120   captions on stills (block centre ~1050), one or two short lines, ≤ 860 px wide
y 1180–1330  CTA chevrons (pointer only)
y > 1152     nothing critical: atmosphere (floor, road, black fade)
```

Stills are generated at 1024×1536 (2:3) and placed in cover (1280×1920); the subject sits in the upper two thirds and the lower third is dark and calm for the captions.

## Palette and typography

| Role | Hex |
|---|---|
| Ground | `#050505` (cards), stills graded to crushed blacks |
| Ink | `#f5f5f2`, shadow `0 4px 18px rgba(0,0,0,.85)` on stills |
| Accent: pain, claim | red `#ff2b2b` (5.3:1 on black) |
| Accent: money (spoken and approved only) | green `#19e36b` |
| Accent: the pivot ("the reason", "the secret") | magenta `#ff3d9a` |
| Accent: desire, the client's world | cyan `#38b6ff` |
| Inverted card | radial `#f2f2f2 → #bdbdbd`, ink `#0a0a0a` |
| Overlay | REC red `#ff2b2b`, scanlines white 3 %, grain 0.16, vignette 0.45 |

One accent per card and at most two accents in a video (e.g. red + green, or red + cyan when a "client's world" section exists).

Fonts: **Anton** (cards, captions, numbers; caps), **Geist 300** caps with `letter-spacing: .14em` (the thin wide label line), Geist 800 for the CTA button. Card words 120–170 px fitted to 900 px with the real TTF; captions on stills 84–100 px; label line 52–64 px.

Grades for the stills (in the generation prompt, finished in CSS): surveillance sepia-olive (`sepia(.45) saturate(.7) contrast(1.18) brightness(.88)`), comic-noir teal and orange, red-and-black monochrome, black and white for a flashback or "the other timeline". Keep one grade family per video; the change of grade is a meaning (present ↔ another timeline).

## Caption system

- Every spoken word is on screen at its start. On stills: groups of 1–3 words (≤ 18 characters per line, ≤ 2 lines) split at punctuation and gaps > 0.5 s, never ending on a function word; on cards the card text **is** the caption of those words (`plan["captions"]` identical to what appears).
- Entry: each word rises inside a line mask (`overflow:hidden`, `yPercent 105 → 0`, 0.16 s, `power3.out`); the group is replaced as a block on the next one (hard cut, no fade), in time with the picture cuts when they coincide.
- Accent words declared in `beats.json`, never more than one per group; the two-weight pair is a group of two lines with its own sizes.
- Contrast: white on black ≥ 15:1; accents ≥ 4.5:1 on black (values above); on stills a soft radial scrim (black .55 at the centre of the caption block) under the text.

## Scene grammar

Generic example (a remarketing ad for a course): frame 0 the mascot sitting in a grimy room under the REC overlay, slam-in, "you kept waiting for the right moment"; cards for each excuse with an icon (hourglass, calendar); a still of a figure looking at the horizon; the mascot stands up (glitch transition) on "the right moment is a story"; a still of eyes in the dark (split frame) on "what is missing is not time"; vacuum → magenta card "today we give you that **reason**"; the mascot points; number card(s) only if the price is spoken and approved; inverted card for the reason the offer is valid now; the mascot's lens flashes at the viewer on "it ends when you close this"; CTA card with chevrons; tail: a silhouette against the light, rock riff, brand mark and button.

| Beat | Picture | Card / caption | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook 0–3 s | mascot or the strongest symbolic still, slam-in | first caption pair from frame 0 | sub boom at 0, bed starts |
| Excuses / diagnosis | 1 still per clause, 2–3 s each | black cards with icon on the named items | whoosh on cuts, soft hit on cards |
| Turn | mascot, new pose | magenta card on the pivot word | vacuum 0.4–0.8 s → sub boom |
| Mechanism / proof | stills, split frame | two-weight pairs for the spoken numbers | riser into the reveal |
| Reason why | inverted card | black type on white | glitch or click |
| Offer | number cards (only spoken and approved) | green on black | coin or impact |
| Close | mascot flash, then CTA card | "TOQUE EM / SAIBA MAIS" + chevrons | camera_flash, click on the tap |
| Tail 4–6 s | atmospheric wide, drift | brand mark + button only | **genre switch**, +3–5 dB |

## Transitions and motion vocabulary

- **Hard cut** by default, on the first syllable of the clause (whoosh −30 dB on picture changes inside a sentence, nothing on cuts that land on a card).
- **Zoom-blur slam** into the first shot and into each mascot shot (scale 1.35 → 1, blur 18 → 0, 0.25 s).
- **Glitch / light leak**: 3–4 frames of RGB split (red/cyan offset 12 px), horizontal slice displacement and an amber over-exposure, between two stills of different grades.
- **Dip to black** with the audio vacuum before a key line (0.3–1.2 s).
- **White flash** (0.12 s) when the mascot's object fires at the viewer.
- Stills: push-in always on, drift 20–40 px, punch-in on stressed words, optional parallax (`heditor depth` on a still) for the mascot.

## Sound design

| Action | Category | gain_db | length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frame 0 slam, card on a claim | `sub_drop` / `bass_impact` | −22 / −24 | — |
| Picture change inside a sentence | `whoosh` (variants alternated) | −30 | 0.6 |
| Mascot shot (REC on) | `glitch` then a short `scan` | −28 / −32 | 0.4 |
| Number card | `trailer_hit` (money: `coins` at −30) | −24 | — |
| Riser into the reveal | `riser` | −28 | 1.2–2.0 |
| Clock, waiting, time | `clock_tick` | −30 | 1.5 |
| Inverted card | `click` or `data_beep` | −28 | — |
| Mascot flash | `camera_flash` | −24 | — |
| CTA tap / button | `mouse_click` / `pop` | −20 / −24 | — |

Music in two parts. **Bed** under the voice from frame 0: dark synth or serious trap, 70–120 BPM, tense and without melody on top of the voice: `music:293` (serious trap), `music:369` (trap, determined), `music:597` (stealthy investigation), `music:568` (tense electronic), `music:871` (dramatic mystery). **Tail**: a contrasting track that starts hard on the first frame after the CTA card, at +3–5 dB over the bed level, with a 1.2 s fade at the end: `music:1117` (confident rock), `music:686` (epic trailer drums), `music:33` (bass, drums, brass), `music:187` (uplifting electronic). Mix profile `presence` (paid ad: music ≈ 15 dB under the voice, `heditor asr-check` required); the vacuum sections are declared silences, the tail is outside the speech.

## CTA design inside the style

On the spoken CTA ("toque em saiba mais…"): the CTA card replaces the picture; "TOQUE EM" (white, 150 px) and "SAIBA MAIS" (accent, 170 px) centred at y ~900; the double chevron (two stacked V strokes, 120 px wide, white) cascades down from y 1180 to 1330 in a 0.6 s loop; on the word "saiba" a finger tap ripples a white "SAIBA MAIS ›" button at y ~1100. After the last word, the 4–6 s tail with the atmospheric wide, the brand mark (top, small) and the same button. `heditor test` fails `test_duration_and_cta` (handle + Follow/Following) by design in paid-ad formats: document it in DESIGN.md, never work around it.

## Generating the stills and the mascot

```
Cinematic dark still, low-key chiaroscuro light, crushed blacks, heavy film grain, strong vignette, <grade family>.
Vertical portrait 2:3, 1024x1536. Scene: <the noun of the clause as a literal or symbolic picture>.
The subject in the upper two thirds; the lower third dark and calm. No text, no letters, no numbers, no logos,
no brands, no readable screens, no recognisable faces (silhouettes, backs or faces in shadow).
```

- **Mascot**: "full-body figure in a black suit, white shirt and black tie, whose head is a <brand object> on a short metal bracket; same character, same suit and same object in every image; <pose>; <room>". Generate the four poses in one session from the first approved image (as reference) so the character stays the same; review each with vision for anatomy, for pseudo-text on the object and for drift of the design.
- One constant style sentence per video (grain, grade, lens); stills of people as backs, silhouettes or faces in shadow; never real people, film frames or brands.
- B-roll from `heditor stock` works for crowds, roads and skies (muted, 1.5–3 s, graded to the same family).

## Implementation notes

- Captions and groups: reuse the phrase splitter of `templates/styles/vox/build.py` (`caption_groups`) with `max_words=3, max_chars=18` and the cards as forced groups (`keep`), so card text and captions stay the same tokens.
- Card text block: a flex column of lines; each line `overflow:hidden`; each word a span that a `FT` moves from `yPercent:105` to `0` at its start. Fit the size with the Anton TTF in Python (never measure the DOM in a timeline callback).
- Slam: `FT(still,{scale:1.35,filter:'blur(18px)'},{scale:1,filter:'blur(0px)',duration:.25,ease:'expo.out'},t)`.
- Glitch: two extra copies of the next still with `mix-blend-mode:screen`, one tinted red and one cyan, offset ±12 px for 3 frames, plus 4 horizontal slices with `clip-path: inset()` shifted 20–60 px (seeded `rnd()`, never `Math.random`).
- REC overlay: a fixed layer shown only during mascot scenes (`S('#rec',{opacity:1},t0)` / `S(...,{opacity:0},t1)`), the dot blinking with a seeded `repeat`; the timecode is text built in Python per frame range, not a live clock.
- Vacuum: split the bed section at t0 and resume at t0+dur with `src` advanced by the same amount (`fade_out .03`, `fade_in .02`); declare it as a silence so `test_music_presence` accepts it.
- Tail: a second music section starting at `voice_end` with its own `track` and `gain_db` +3–5 over the bed; `plan["duck_open"]` covers the tail.

## Pitfalls to expect

- **Generated mascots drift** (the object changes shape, the tie changes colour) across poses: generate the poses from the approved first image as a reference and reject drifted ones.
- **Pseudo-text in stills** (signs, book covers, whiteboards in English) reads as careless AI: ask for no text and check every still with vision.
- **ASR hallucinations in the tail**: Whisper writes song lyrics or "subtitles by…" over the music after the last word; check the last segments before computing `voice_end`.
- **Cards that only repeat the caption** make the video read as a slideshow: a card is a claim, a number or the pivot, never a filler line.
- **Too many accents**: more than one coloured word per card, or more than two accent colours, kills the "one word burns" effect.
- **Scarcity copied from the reference** ("the system knows you saw it") is a claim nobody can verify; leave it out even if the script has it, and ask the user.

## Variations

- **Comic noir**: every still an inked graphic-novel panel (halftone, teal and orange), captions small and centred; for fables and "imagine a world" scripts.
- **Surveillance**: sepia-olive stills and the REC overlay as the frame of the whole hook; for remarketing and "we know you saw it" (only when true).
- **Red room**: red-and-black monochrome stills, red accent only; for B2B service ads with numbers.
- **Light card variant**: two or three inverted cards for a mechanism with steps (the steps are the only light frames).
- **Long form**: chapters as black cards with the two-weight pair, the mascot as the chapter opener, stills at 4–6 s each with the same push-in, the tail music only at the very end.
